# What Jev can tell of a chapter

Before settling what to ask Jev about a book, it was tried on chapters whose answers were known beforehand: what it reads well, where it stops telling things apart, and what moves its answers besides the text. The questions used here are trials, and the numbers got with them are no valuation of any book.

    npm run judge -- books/king-solomons-mines.epub --sections 4-24        # says what it would send and cost
    npm run judge -- books/king-solomons-mines.epub --sections 4-24 --go   # sends it
    node experiments/books/jev/wording.ts                                   # an experiment: it spends when run

A score is where a passage falls among the levels of a scale, from 0; a noul is the probability of a yes. Everything below was run on 2026-09-19 with `jev-1.13.0`: 108 calls, 548,000 tokens, $0.023 at list price, some 90 seconds in all. The passages told worse on purpose cost another $0.007 of `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`.

## What it does well

**It reads a whole chapter, and more.** The longest chapter of the book, 6,200 words, was read as well as the shortest. Five chapters sent as one text, 22,000 words and 30,000 tokens, which is all a call takes, were still read to the end: asked whether the text ends with something left unresolved, Jev said 0.14 of the three chapters that end with the battle won and 0.97 of the five that end a chapter later, as it had of those two chapters alone.

**It reads what is said, not what it looks like.** A chapter of Austen that is a letter has 88% of its text inside quotation marks, and got 0.5 of 4 for conversation; the first chapter of the same book, 76% inside them and a conversation indeed, 3.1. Over the 35 passages where both were had, how much conversation Jev said there was and how much text code found inside quotation marks agree in rank by 0.74; how long it said the sentences were and the words a sentence code counted, by 0.76 over 39.

**It says the same twice.** The 99 answers of the first run, asked for again, came back 0.007 apart on average on a scale of 0 to 1, and 0.05 at most. A difference smaller than that is no difference.

**It tells good writing from bad.** Asked whether a passage is finely written, the work of a writer in full command of the craft:

| Passage                                                 | Yes        |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Three chapters of _Pride and Prejudice_                 | 0.94–0.95  |
| Three chapters of _Don Quijote_, in Spanish             | 0.92–0.94  |
| Four passages of _King Solomon's Mines_                 | 0.74–0.89  |
| The royal licence printed at the front of _Don Quijote_ | 0.23       |
| Four chapters of _Irene Iddesleigh_                     | 0.19–0.35  |
| A passage of Haggard and a chapter of Austen, told dull | 0.19, 0.48 |
| The same two, told overdone                             | 0.71, 0.49 |

Between a passage and its rewrites what happens is the same, and so said Jev: the danger the characters are in came to 1.10, 1.04 and 1.15 of 3. What moved was owed to the writing.

**Side by side, it is steadier still.** Which of two passages is the better written was answered the same both ways round in all seven pairs: Austen over _Irene Iddesleigh_ and every original over its rewrites with 0.99 or more, but for Haggard over his overdone rewrite, 0.84 and 0.70. Where there was nothing to choose, as in which of two drawing-room scenes has more danger, it leant to the first.

**A book costs half a cent.** _King Solomon's Mines_ whole, a chapter a call and eleven questions each, is 132,000 tokens, $0.0056 and 15 seconds. The questions weigh: each adds some 110 tokens to every call, which doubles the cost of a piece of 1,000 words.

## Where it gets stuck

**The words of a question move the middle of its scale.** Chapters at either end of a scale stay there however it is worded: the battle, 3.00 and 2.98 of 3 for danger; Austen, 0 and 0. In between, a chapter can move a whole level, because Jev reads a level to the letter: the eve of the battle, where a herald threatens the heroes with torture, fell from 2.58 to 1.54 when a level came to say "threatened with violence that does not come". A score is only to be set beside those got with the same words, which is what the hash of the questions in every record is for.

**A short scale tops out.** How much happens came to 2.8 of 3 or more in seventeen of the twenty chapters of a novel of adventures. With two more levels above, the last of them kept for a story turned around, five of those chapters spread from 2.9 to 4.6 of 5, the battle on top. Numbers for levels, the other way, flatten a scale into all or nothing: the march through the desert and the battle, 1.8 and 3.0 with levels in words, came to 2.95 and 2.99.

**A whole chapter is not the mean of its parts.** For what either is there or is not, like danger, a chapter scores near the most dangerous of its pieces: the desert, 1.0 in four pieces and 2.0 in the last, 1.8 whole. For what is a share, like conversation, it scores near their mean. Pieces tell more apart: the humour of the battle chapter, 1.0 of 2 whole, runs from 0.3 to 1.0 in its pieces.

**Ornament impresses it.** The overdone rewrite of Haggard was still finely written by 0.71, and its wording as much the writer's own as the original's. Other questions catch it, overdone at 2.00 of 2 and easy to follow at 0.4 of 2, and so does setting it beside the original. No one question of taste stands alone.

**What it is told of a text moves what it says of it.** With "critics have called it the worst written in the language" beside it, Austen's chapter fell from 0.95 to 0.53 as finely written; with another author's name, to 0.68. Praise did less for _Irene Iddesleigh_: 0.19 to 0.27. Questions that describe barely moved: easy to follow, 1.64 to 1.59. Jev is to be given the text and nothing about it: no title, no author.

**Old prose is hard prose.** Cervantes came out hard to follow, 0.6 to 1.0 of 2, and overdone, 1.6 to 1.8 of 2; so did the denser chapters of Austen. That is the distance between the text and a reader of today, which is real and is not a fault of the writing.

**What came before does not matter to it.** With what the reader knows so far beside the chapter, the 63 answers about three chapters moved by 0.02 on average on a scale of 0 to 1, and 0.12 at most. For questions about the chapter in hand, the notes neither help nor get in the way.

**Spanish is no obstacle.** With the questions in English, the royal licence of _Don Quijote_ came out as nothing happening, 0.2 of 2 for humour and 0.06 of 3 for wording of the writer's own; its chapters, 1.9 for humour, 2.7 to 2.9 for wording, and the windmills 2.1 of 3 for danger.
